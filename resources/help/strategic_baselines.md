## Context & Definition

A Strategic Baseline is a version-locked snapshot of your approved Business Objectives, Business Needs, and Scope Boundaries at a specific milestone in time (e.g., v1.0-Baseline). In BABOK terms, a baseline is the standard against which all future requirements, designs, and changes are measured. It represents the state of requirements that have reached formal consensus.

## Why It Matters

Requirements change constantly as stakeholders learn more about the system. Without version freezing, project teams experience "scope bleeding" (or scope creep), where targets shift daily without formal agreement or budget adjustments. The Strategic Baseline draws a line in the sand. It allows teams to lock down an agreed-upon scope so that any subsequent modifications must be formally evaluated for their impact on time, cost, and value.

## How to Use

1. **Gain Consensus First:** Do not lock a baseline in isolation. Ensure you have explicit agreement (sign-off) from the stakeholders assigned in your Stakeholders Matrix.
2. **Freeze Approved Artifacts:** Once the initial strategy and scope are approved, transition their status and lock/freeze the agreed scope at that milestone. Subsequent changes go through formal change evaluation rather than ad-hoc edits.
3. **Trigger Impact Analysis for Changes:** If a stakeholder requests a change after the baseline is locked, do not just edit the text. Use the baseline to enforce a formal Change Request (CR) process, documenting the variance and evaluating what downstream features will be impacted.
4. **Audit Historical Versions:** Compare current iterative changes against the original baseline during project retrospectives to understand how much the business strategy evolved during development.

## The Bigger Picture & Downstream Links

- **Upstream:** Encapsulates and protects the foundational strategy: Business Objectives, Business Needs, and defined Solution Scope.
- **Downstream:** Acts as a protective shield for downstream Execution. It ensures that developers (working on Solution Requirements, BDD Scenarios, and Sprints) are building against a stable, approved target rather than a moving one.
- **The Rule of Integrity:** A baseline is only as strong as its approvals. Never baseline requirements that still have unresolved conflicts among key stakeholders.
