## Context & Definition

A Business Need is a specific problem, gap, or opportunity that prevents an enterprise from reaching its strategic Business Objectives. While objectives dictate the destination (the "where we want to be"), business needs pinpoint the specific roadblocks currently standing in the way (the "why we can't get there today").

In practice: “Manual phone and email inquiries lead to high error rates and delayed spare parts procurement.”

## Why It Matters

Jumping straight to solutions is the most common and expensive trap for business analysts. Stakeholders love to ask for solutions, like a "new reporting dashboard" or a "cloud portal." Capturing the Business Need forces a rigorous, mandatory pause: you must articulate exactly what is broken in the current operational model before anyone writes a single user story. It ensures the team spends its budget on solving the underlying operational problem rather than rushing into a preconceived—and potentially useless—technical solution.

## How to Use

1. **Contrast the States:** Detail the painful reality of current operational friction (Current State) versus the intended optimized operational model (Future State).
2. **Apply the Problem Formula:** Formulate the need clearly using a strict Problem + Impact structure. (e.g., "The current bottleneck of [X] causes [Negative Impact Y], preventing us from achieving [Objective Z]").
3. **Keep it Problem-Focused (No Tech allowed):** Focus exclusively on operational bottlenecks, regulatory gaps, capacity limits, or market opportunities. If your stated Business Need includes words like "button," "database," "API," or "software," you have written a requirement. Delete it and try again.
4. **Anchor to Strategy:** Tie every single business need directly to the high-level Business Objective it threatens or blocks.

## The Bigger Picture & Downstream Links

- **Upstream (The Justification):** Justified and anchored exclusively by Business Objectives. A business need without a parent objective is just a localized complaint that doesn't serve the enterprise.
- **Downstream (The Drivers):** Directly spawns Stakeholder Requirements, Solution Scope boundaries, and eventually Functional Features.
- **The Rule of Integrity:** Any stakeholder requirement, software feature, or user story that cannot trace back upstream to an active, approved Business Need is an unapproved candidate for scope creep and should be challenged.

**Practical tip:** When a stakeholder says, "I need an automated email system," they are giving you a solution, not a need. Your job is to ask, "What business problem does an automated email system solve for you today?" The answer to that question is your Business Need. Fall in love with the problem, not the software.
