title: Business Needs

## Context & Definition

A Business Need is the enterprise-level **why** for change: a problem, gap, or opportunity that drives the initiative. Business needs sit at the top of the requirements hierarchy. They answer: *Why must the enterprise change at all?*

In practice: “Manual phone and email spare-parts inquiries cause high error rates and delayed procurement.”

## Why It Matters

Jumping straight to solutions is the most common and expensive trap for business analysts. Stakeholders love to ask for solutions, like a "new reporting dashboard" or a "cloud portal." Capturing the Business Need forces a rigorous, mandatory pause: you must articulate exactly what is broken (or which opportunity is untapped) before anyone writes objectives, stakeholder requirements, or user stories. It ensures the team spends its budget on the underlying enterprise driver rather than a preconceived technical solution.

## How to Use

1. **Contrast the States:** Detail the painful reality of current operational friction (Current State) versus the intended optimized operational model (Future State).
2. **Apply the Problem Formula:** Formulate the need clearly using a strict Problem + Impact structure (e.g., "The current bottleneck of [X] causes [Negative Impact Y]").
3. **Keep it Problem-Focused (No Tech allowed):** Focus exclusively on operational bottlenecks, regulatory gaps, capacity limits, or market opportunities. If your stated Business Need includes words like "button," "database," "API," or "software," you have written a requirement. Delete it and try again.
4. **Derive the What Next:** Once the why is clear, define Business Objectives (measurable outcomes) that would satisfy this need.

## The Bigger Picture & Downstream Links

- **Upstream (The Apex):** None within the need spine. Business Needs are justified by enterprise strategy, market reality, and the Strategic Baseline — not by other BAssist entities.
- **Downstream (The Drivers):** Each Business Need drives one or more Business Objectives (the measurable **what**), Scope Items, and — through those objectives — Stakeholder Needs and Solution Requirements.
- **The Rule of Integrity:** Any stakeholder requirement, software feature, or user story that cannot trace back upstream to an active, approved Business Need is an unapproved candidate for scope creep and should be challenged.

**Practical tip:** When a stakeholder says, "I need an automated email system," they are giving you a solution, not a need. Your job is to ask, "What business problem does an automated email system solve for you today?" The answer to that question is your Business Need. Fall in love with the problem, not the software.
